\section*{Introduction}

% CLAIM_ID: INT-01
Nutrient-profile models rate the nutritional quality of individual foods for defined public-health applications \cite{labonte2018nutrientprofiles}.
% CLAIM_ID: INT-02
Systematic nutrient-profile models use explicit, transparent rules to compare foods for defined purposes \cite{scarborough2007developing}.
Together, these properties define the shared food-level reference used here.

% CLAIM_ID: INT-03
Postprandial glycaemic responses to the same foods can differ substantially between individuals \cite{zeevi2015personalized}.
% CLAIM_ID: INT-04
GMNPS retains a shared food-level reference and represents personal information as a separate bounded calibration.
This distinction separates a common food-quality reference from the additional
information needed to study individual variation and makes the permitted
movement explicit.

% CLAIM_ID: INT-05
GMNPS applies bounded changes to native attributes before domain recomposition.
% CLAIM_ID: INT-06
Gut microbiome features have been studied as candidate inputs for personalized dietary-response models \cite{zeevi2015personalized}.
The microbiome is used here as a feature source rather than a causal mechanism
or a sufficient basis for dietary decisions.

Food Compass 2.0, the broad nutrient profiling baseline, was used here.
% CLAIM_ID: INT-07
The GMNPS method uses bounded attribute-level calibration with locked inputs.
% CLAIM_ID: INT-08
The current evidence tier is computational feasibility. The prespecified
evidence chain separates method establishment, evidence authorization, data
availability, a correctly specified synthetic positive-control and the
boundary on empirical interpretation.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{Bounded native-attribute calibration established the scoring object}

The GMNPS method computes bounded attribute-level scores from locked inputs.
For each person, normalized microbiome-derived nutrient calibration inputs are
combined with food-specific exposures and author-specified nutrient-to-attribute
allocation weights. Available expert feedback informed nutrient-channel content,
not the numerical allocation weights.
The resulting response changes native attribute points before the selected Food Compass domains are dynamically recomposed. Calibration therefore occurs before domain recomposition rather than after completion of the 1-to-100 value.

The primary attribute movement is capped at 20\% of each attribute's published
point range. Recomposition retains food-specific baseline contributions outside
the selected domains and then limits the final deviation from the Food Compass
2.0 anchor to \(\pm 12\) points. Population and per-food centring are absent.
At exactly zero normalized response, every calibrated attribute and the final
value equal their corresponding baselines to the locked numerical tolerance.
This identity and the two caps are designed computational invariants rather than
observations of population preservation.

\subsection*{A fail-closed audit fixed the evidence tier at computational feasibility}

The evidence gate implements a computational, fail-closed design. It authorizes
only computational feasibility, a correctly specified synthetic positive-
control, audited data unavailability and the fail-closed design itself. The
current decision did not pass because the required real validation artifacts
were absent. This decision records evidence eligibility and authorization; it
does not measure predictive performance.

\subsection*{Observed outcomes and production execution were unavailable}

Eligible observed participant-by-meal outcomes are unavailable in the audited
data. Access to the controlled archive had not been granted, and the audited materials
did not establish an eligible endpoint table, verified meal key, or verified
participant-to-microbiome linkage. Public microbiome predictors and aggregate
food ranks were not treated as substitutes for participant-by-meal outcomes.

Production execution has not been performed for the 9,234-food universe. The
trusted Food Compass and FNDDS artifact chain required by the locked procedure
was incomplete, and no authorized production person-food panel was generated.
Consequently, no population rank preservation, category transition, food-group
heterogeneity, direct-response, GMrepo, ZOE rank or knowledge-path statistic is
reported in the main text.

\subsection*{Available expert feedback informed nutrient-channel revisions}

Available expert feedback informed nutrient-channel revisions and supports
content review only. The available comments informed treatment of carbohydrate,
zinc, copper and vitamin A as retinol activity equivalents. This content review
does not establish outcome validity or superiority of the revised channel
assignment.

\section*{Discussion}

The defensible contribution is a reproducible formulation of bounded
personalized calibration at the native-attribute level. By applying calibration
before domain recomposition, the formulation retains published attribute
ranges, ratio applicability, dynamic top-\(k\) selection and a food-specific
fixed residual. The 20\% native-attribute cap, \(\pm 12\)-point final cap,
absence of score centring and exact zero-response identity are safety properties
of the design. They are not evidence that population rankings, health outcomes
or individual responses are preserved in real data.

Earlier precision-nutrition studies combined dietary, host and microbiome
information to characterize inter-individual responses
\cite{zeevi2015personalized,berry2020postprandial,asnicar2021microbiome}.
The present contribution addresses a different problem: how an individual
calibration layer can remain legible inside a shared nutrient-profile reference.
The correctly specified synthetic positive-control, shown in Supplementary
Fig. S1 and summarized in Supplementary Table S2, shows that the locked code
recovers a mapping programmed into its own data-generating process and loses
that recovery after random or Sattolo-deranged assignment. Because generator
and implementation share the mapping, this evidence diagnoses implementation
fidelity but not biological misspecification or real-world response prediction.

Available expert feedback further constrains interpretation. It supports
content review of four nutrient-channel decisions, but it does not establish
outcome validity, external validity, readiness for clinical use or superiority of an
expert-revised mask.

The present evidence package is not ready for an empirical Article. Eligible
observed participant-by-meal outcomes, a trusted production food run and
registry-bound supporting analyses are absent, so the main quantitative
evidence needed for response validity, population behaviour and generalization
cannot be assembled. GMNPS does not establish external validity. No analysis evaluated clinical utility or dietary recommendations.

A future empirical programme should preserve the frozen method while adding a
trusted production food bundle, eligible observed outcomes and held-out analyses
whose splits, inference units, intervals, multiplicity control, leakage checks
and provenance are fixed before outcome inspection. Multimodal inputs and
longitudinal sampling could then test whether calibration remains stable as host,
dietary and microbiome states change \cite{david2014diet,asnicar2026gut}.
Clinical deployment would additionally require prospective safety assessment,
decision-curve analysis and reporting appropriate to high-risk prediction tools
\cite{collins2015tripod,wolff2019probast,collins2024tripodAi}. Digital gut twins
remain a future experimental construct for counterfactual testing, not a label
for the completed synthetic positive-control \cite{gkouskou2020digitaltwins}.

\section*{Methods}

\subsection*{Study design and evidence boundary}

This work specified a computational calibration procedure and audited the evidence available to evaluate it. Food Compass 2.0 supplied the shared food-level baseline \cite{barrett2024foodcompass2}. The locked
method version was fixed before validation access and separated development-only
normalization from later scoring inputs. No eligible observed participant-by-
meal outcome table or authorized production food bundle entered the present
work. Full algorithms, provenance requirements, missingness rules and planned
empirical estimands are reported in Supplementary Methods.

\subsection*{Scoring object and native attribute rules}

Food composition exposures are expressed per 100 kcal unless a published rule
uses percentage energy, a ratio, a binary indicator or NOVA class. Each
calculated Food Compass attribute is scored on its published native point range.
Ratio attributes are removed from the domain denominator when their published
applicability gate fails; unavailable attributes are not zero-filled. Selected
domains are aggregated from calculated attribute points using their effective
weights, including dynamic top-five selection for vitamins and minerals and
top-three selection for specific lipids.

\subsection*{Microbiome-derived calibration interface}

Let \(M_i\) denote a person's centred log-ratio genus-abundance vector. A sparse
logistic health index \(H\) is fitted in development data using available
healthy and non-healthy labels. For nutrient \(n\), an author-specified
nutrient-to-genus direction \(P_n\) combines a curated association matrix with
the sign and magnitude of fitted genus coefficients. The raw calibration input
is the central finite-difference contrast
\[
b_{in}=\frac{H(M_i+\delta P_n)-H(M_i-\delta P_n)}{2\delta}.
\]
This construction uses disease labels only to fit the development health index;
it does not quantify effects of nutrient exposure. Evaluation cohorts must be
excluded from health-index fitting and all subsequent normalization. The
present evidence package did not execute this production evaluation chain.

Development-only medians and robust scales are frozen by nutrient, and
later scoring inputs are transformed as
\[
z_{in}=\tanh\!\left(\frac{b_{in}-m_n}{2s_n}\right).
\]
For food \(j\), normalized exposure \(e_{jna}\) and author-specified allocation
\(w_{na}\) map nutrient inputs to native attribute \(a\):
\[
r_{ija}=\sum_n z_{in}e_{jna}w_{na}.
\]
Allocation weights for one nutrient sum to one. The input is a computational calibration feature. Causal effects were not assessed.

\subsection*{Bounded attribute calibration and recomposition}

For published point range \([l_a,h_a]\), baseline point \(p_{ja}\) and primary
fraction \(c=0.20\), the calibrated native point is
\[
p_{ija}=\operatorname{clip}\!\left[
p_{ja}+c(h_a-l_a)\tanh\!\left(\frac{r_{ija}}{2}\right),l_a,h_a\right].
\]
The selected domains are then recomputed from personalized native points. A
food-specific fixed residual retains the official-anchor contribution outside
those recomputed domains. If \(F_j\) is the Food Compass 2.0 anchor and
\(F^{\mathrm{native}}_{ij}\) the recomposed score, the final value is
\[
S_{ij}=\operatorname{clip}_{[1,100]}\!\left[
F_j+\operatorname{clip}_{[-12,12]}
\left(F^{\mathrm{native}}_{ij}-F_j\right)\right].
\]
Population and per-food centring are absent. A zero normalized response
returns the anchor exactly.

\subsection*{Nutrient-channel content review}

The author-specified mapping assigns nutrient inputs to native Food Compass attributes
and records primary, applicability-only, explanatory, sensitivity-proxy and
excluded roles. Available external feedback informed revisions concerning
carbohydrate, zinc, copper and vitamin A as retinol activity equivalents. The
feedback was used only for nutrient-channel content review. Numerical
nutrient-to-attribute allocation weights were author-specified. No outcome was
available for tuning the mapping.

\subsection*{Correctly specified synthetic positive-control}

The positive-control generator fixed the Food Compass baseline component,
person-level capacities, attribute exposures, programmed native-attribute
effects and independent noise before scoring. Five independent simulation seeds
were used, each containing 24 synthetic people and 12 synthetic foods. The
formal run used only the primary 20\% native-attribute cap and \(\pm 12\)-point
final cap. Random assignment and deterministic Sattolo derangement were controls
within the same evidence family. Detailed outputs appear only in the Supplementary Information.

\subsection*{Evidence gate}

The evidence gate requires path-bound artifacts, a valid run-level method lock,
disjoint development and test subjects, eligible observed outcomes and the
prespecified subject-held-out comparisons before any direct-response claim can
be authorized. Synthetic, aggregate, predictor-only and unexecuted supporting
resources cannot upgrade the tier. In the absence of the required artifacts,
the gate returns computational feasibility and an explicit blocker list.
