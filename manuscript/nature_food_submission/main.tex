\title[Personalized calibration transforms NPS]{Personalized calibration transforms nutrient profiling systems for precision nutrition}

\abstract{Nutrient profiling systems provide shared food-level guidance for population nutrition, but their static scores cannot express the metabolic heterogeneity that motivates precision nutrition. We introduce GMNPS, an anchored calibration framework that adds a bounded personalized deviation to a nutrient-profiling prior rather than replacing that prior with a fully individualized recommender. Food Compass 2.0 was used as the selected baseline prior, and gut microbiome-informed nutrient responses served as a proof-of-concept calibration layer. In a 9,234-food scoring run, population-mean GMNPS remained nearly identical to the baseline prior (Spearman \(=0.999839\)), with 79 broad-category shifts at the population mean (0.856\%). The expert-revised v4 mask improved anchoring relative to the previous aggregate mask output (Spearman \(=0.999839\) versus 0.808736) and reduced mean personalized variance (26.27 versus 417.38). GMNPS also expressed bounded food-level heterogeneity with channel-attributed patterns across food groups. In a known-ground-truth digital-gut-twin benchmark, anchored GMNPS recovered more personalized residual structure than unanchored microbiome scoring (Spearman \(=0.396\) versus 0.248) while preserving the population NPS ranking (Spearman \(=0.999965\) versus 0.932292). These results support computational feasibility and biological plausibility for precision-ready nutrient profiling, while leaving clinical efficacy and individual-level human response prediction to future retrospective or prospective validation.}

\keywords{nutrient profiling; precision nutrition; gut microbiome; personalized calibration; digital gut twin; Food Compass}
